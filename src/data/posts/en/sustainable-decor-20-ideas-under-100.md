Sustainable home decor does not have to mean expensive organic fabrics, imported wooden furniture, or designer pieces that cost more than your furniture budget for the entire year. In fact, some of the most beautiful, planet-friendly updates to your home cost almost nothing and can be done with things you already own or can find cheaply at local thrift stores. In 2026, sustainable decor is less about what you buy and more about how you think. It is about reusing before replacing, choosing secondhand first, buying locally when you can, and being intentional about every piece you bring into your home. This guide shares twenty ideas that will make your space feel warmer, more personal, and more environmentally responsible, all while keeping your budget well under a hundred dollars — or a hundred reais, depending on where you live.

One of the easiest sustainable decor updates is rearranging what you already own. Before you buy anything, shuffle your furniture around. Move the sofa to a different wall. Swap the side table from the bedroom to the living room. Hang a piece of art in a new room. Rearranging costs nothing, but it completely changes the energy of a space. If rearranging reveals that you have too much of something or that something is genuinely scratched and no longer functional, then you can start thinking about replacements. But always start with what you have.

Thrifting is the backbone of affordable sustainable decor. Thrift stores, consignment shops, flea markets, and online marketplaces like Facebook Marketplace are full of solid wood furniture, ceramic vases, woven baskets, framed art, and vintage textiles that would cost three to five times more new. A solid oak side table for twenty reais will outlast a particleboard table from a big-box store that costs five hundred. A vintage ceramic planter for ten reais is more interesting than a plastic pot you would buy new at a garden store. Shop secondhand first, and if you cannot find what you need, then consider buying new from ethical brands.

Upcycling furniture is the next level of sustainable decor. If you have a wooden dresser that looks dated or a coffee table with a scratched top, you can transform it with paint, new hardware, or even a simple linen cloth. Sanding a rough surface and applying one to two coats of chalk paint or matte latex paint costs less than fifty reais in materials and can make a piece look entirely new. Swap the old brass or silver pulls for matte black or brass handles from a hardware store for five to ten reais. If you are renting and cannot paint, peel-and-stick wallpaper on the back of a bookshelf or the side of a cabinet adds personality without permanence. Peel-and-stick tiles can refresh a kitchen backsplash for under a hundred reais, and you can remove them when you move.

Textiles add warmth and texture faster than almost any other decor element. Swap your throw pillows for covers in natural fabrics like linen, cotton, or wool. Thrift stores sell beautiful vintage textiles — embroidered pillowcases, woven blankets, crochet doilies — for tiny prices. Hang a woven wall hanging above your bed instead of buying new art. Drape a cozy blanket over the arm of your couch. Put a small rug made from natural fibers like jute, sisal, or recycled cotton in your entryway. Rugs made from recycled materials or natural fibers are widely available and usually cost between fifty and a hundred reais. A rug instantly makes an apartment feel like a home rather than a rental.

Plants are arguably the cheapest, most impactful decor update you can make. A snake plant costs ten to twenty reais and thrives with almost no care. A pothos in a mason jar wrapped in twine looks beautiful on a shelf or windowsill. A peace lily cleans your air and produces white blooms for a fraction of the cost of fresh flowers. Visit a local nursery or even a grocery store that sells houseplants. If you are on an extremely tight budget, propagate plants from cuttings of friends' plants — pothos, philodendron, and succulents all root easily in water. Group plants in mismatched thrifted pots for a curated, bohemian look.

Lighting transforms a room more than paint or furniture. Swap harsh overhead lighting for warm LED bulbs that mimic natural sunlight. Add a table lamp with a fabric or rattan shade in the corner of your living room. Hang fairy lights or string lights above a headboard or around a window for a cozy glow. A rustic wooden lantern or a thrifted brass lamp on your nightstand adds character. Thrift stores often have vintage lamps that just need a new cord or a new lampshade. Replacing an old shade with a natural linen or wicker shade costs thirty to fifty reais and updates the entire piece.

For walls, skip the expensive printed art and make your own. Frame a dried flower border, a map from a trip, or fabric scraps in mismatched thrifted frames. Create a gallery wall with postcards, family photos, and small paintings. Buy a cheap stretched canvas, paint it with leftover house paint in your favorite color, and let it dry. Frame old concert tickets, children's artwork, or pressed leaves. A gallery wall does not need to be perfectly curated. In fact, a slightly chaotic mix of sizes and subjects feels more personal and interesting than a perfectly matched set of prints. The total cost is under a hundred reais if you use thrifted frames.

Swapping hardware is one of the easiest ways to update a kitchen or bathroom without renovation. Old brass or chrome handles on cabinets and drawers can be replaced with matte black, brass, or bronze pulls for around five to ten reais per handle. If your faucet looks outdated, you can replace the entire fixture for under a hundred reais or just replace the aerator for a few reais to improve water flow. Swapping shower curtain rings or adding a shower curtain liner with a natural texture updates the bathroom instantly.

Repurposing glass jars is a classic sustainable hack that never gets old. Jam jars, pasta sauce jars, and pickle jars make excellent storage containers for pantry staples like rice, pasta, spices, and flour. Remove the labels by soaking them in warm soapy water, then use them as drinking glasses, utensil holders on your counter, or candle holders. A little twine or lace around the lid makes them look intentional and crafty rather than like you are saving money. Bulk stores often have spices and dry goods that you can refill jars with, reducing packaging waste forever.

Baskets are the unsung heroes of small space organization. Woven baskets made from seagrass, jute, or recycled plastic corral blankets, toys, magazines, and laundry. They look natural and cozy rather than like ugly plastic bins. Place a large basket beside your couch for throw blankets, a medium basket on your shelf for miscellaneous items, and a small basket on your nightstand for charging cables. Baskets cost between fifteen and sixty reais depending on size and material. Look for them at thrift stores, home goods stores, or local artisan markets.

Sustainable swaps in the kitchen go beyond decor. Replace plastic wrap and zipper bags with beeswax wraps or reusable silicone bags. They last for years, reduce plastic waste, and look much cleaner on counters or in lunch bags. Switch to reusable produce bags made from organic cotton or recycled mesh for grocery shopping. Use a French press or reusable coffee filters instead of disposable pods. A simple wooden dish rack or a bamboo dish brush replaces plastic versions that break and need replacing. These small kitchen changes cost less than a hundred reais total and make your kitchen feel more intentional.

If you have outdoor space, even a tiny balcony, you can create a green haven with recycled materials. Old wooden pallets become vertical planters or small tables. Plastic bottles cut in half become seedling starters. A rope suspended between two wall hooks can hold small potted succulents. A vintage wicker chair from a thrift store costs twenty to forty reais and becomes your new reading spot. String lights across the balcony ceiling make evening drinks feel magical.

Creating a gallery of memories with printed photos is deeply personal and sustainable. In 2026, we take millions of digital photos but almost never print them. Order prints from a local photo lab or print them at a kiosk and arrange them in small frames on a shelf or a memory wall. Mix prints with small plants and trinkets for a personal vignette. Alternatively, print photos on matte paper and clip them to a string with mini wooden clothespins. This creates a flexible gallery that you can change whenever you want.

A reading nook can be created for very little money if you are willing to get creative. A large floor cushion, a small rug, a floor lamp, and a bookshelf or a stack of books turn any corner into a cozy reading spot. If you have a window seat or a space beside a window, a cushioned bench is perfect. Build a simple bench from wooden crates or old drawers for under fifty reais and add a cushion. Reading nooks create a sense of comfort and retreat, which is exactly what every small apartment needs.

Washi tape and Command strips are renters' best friends. Washi tape comes in every color and pattern and peels off walls without leaving residue. Use it to create geometric patterns on a wall, frame photos, label storage bins, or hang small pieces of art. Command strips let you hang heavy items like shelves, clocks, and mirrors without damaging the walls. Both are available at dollar stores or stationery shops for under thirty reais. They turn any rental into a personalized space without costing your security deposit.

Building a sustainable home salon or vanity area is totally possible on a budget. A thrifted wooden dressing table, a thrifted mirror, and some small baskets for makeup and skincare create a functional vanity. Use glass jars for cotton pads, Q-tips, and skincare samples. A small stick-on LED mirror light above the vanity provides flattering light for makeup application. The total cost is under a hundred reais if you shop secondhand, and you avoid buying a new vanity made from particleboard.

For the bathroom, sustainable swaps include switching from disposable cotton rounds to reusable bamboo rounds, using a safety razor instead of disposable plastic razors, and replacing plastic shower bottles with refillable glass or stainless steel bottles. A simple teak bath caddy rests over the edge of your tub and holds a book, a candle, and a glass of wine for the cost of a cheap dinner. A potted eucalyptus branch hung from your shower head releases a spa-like scent when it gets wet and costs about fifteen reais.

Simple DIY candles from soy wax and essential oils cost less than store-bought candles and reduce plastic waste. Buy soy wax flakes online or at a craft store, melt them in a double boiler, pour into thrifted glass jars or small ceramic cups, add cotton wicks and a few drops of lavender or eucalyptus essential oil, and let them set. A homemade candle made with love and natural ingredients is nicer than anything you can buy, and the whole process costs under thirty reais. They also make incredible gifts.

The final sustainable decor idea is to slow down. We live in a culture of fast decor, where every season we feel like we need to change everything. Sustainable living is about resisting that urge. Choose pieces that you love deeply and that will last for years. Buy less, choose well, and care for what you own. A home filled with a few well-loved things is infinitely nicer than a home filled with things you bought because they were cheap. In 2026, the most sustainable house is the one you do not feel the need to constantly redecorate.
