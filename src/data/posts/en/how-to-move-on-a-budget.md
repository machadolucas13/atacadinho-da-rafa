Moving is one of the most stressful life events you can experience, right up there with breakups and changing jobs. It is expensive, physically exhausting, emotionally draining, and usually full of surprises — like the box you packed in the bathroom that you cannot find until three days after you have already unpacked everything else. In 2026, moving costs keep climbing as fuel prices, truck rental rates, and moving labor fees increase. A local move that would have cost three thousand reais a few years ago might now cost six thousand or more. But a budget move does not have to mean a low-quality, disorganized experience. With a little planning, creativity, and a willingness to do some work yourself, you can move safely and comfortably for a fraction of the typical price. This guide covers everything from packing to your first night in the new place while keeping costs low.

The biggest decision you will make is whether to hire a moving company, rent a truck and do it yourself, or combine both by hiring labor only. Professional movers are the most expensive option but also the least stressful because they pack, load, transport, and unload everything for you. For people who are short on time, have health issues, or are moving a long distance with a lot of furniture, the cost might be worth it. For everyone else on a budget, renting a moving truck and doing it yourself with help from friends is the way to go. U-Haul, local rental companies, and truck-sharing platforms offer small-to-medium trucks starting at around two hundred to four hundred reais per day for local moves. If you bribe your friends with pizza and drinks, your only labor cost is gratitude — and maybe a favor returned later. For long-distance moves, compare the cost of renting a truck and driving it yourself versus flying and hiring movers on the other end. Sometimes flying and shipping boxes is cheaper than paying for gas, hotels, and tolls for a multi-day drive.

Packing materials are another area where you can save hundreds of reais. Moving companies and office supply stores sell boxes, bubble wrap, packing tape, and paper for hundreds of reais, but you do not need to buy almost any of it. Ask local grocery stores, liquor stores, and retailers for free boxes. They usually stack them behind the store and throw them away every evening. Most boxes are sturdy enough for moving if you do not put anything extremely heavy in them. Use towels, bed sheets, and clothing to wrap fragile items instead of buying bubble wrap. A wine glass wrapped in a thick sweater is just as safe as one wrapped in bubble wrap. Use suitcases and duffel bags for clothes instead of cardboard boxes. Suitcases have wheels and handles, which makes them the cheapest and easiest packing option for clothes you do not want to fold. Use old newspapers or junk mail to fill gaps in boxes, and collect plastic bags from grocery stores to protect electronics and liquids from leaks.

Labeling your boxes is one of the simplest things you can do that will save you hours of frustration later. Buy a pack of colored stickers or use colored Sharpies to designate which room each box belongs to. Red for the kitchen, blue for the bedroom, green for the bathroom, and so on. On each box, write the room and a brief list of contents — kitchen dishes, winter coats, books — and whether the contents are fragile. If you really want to impress, number your boxes and keep a notebook with the contents of each number. This way, you know exactly where to find your coffee maker or your charger on day one. Do not just write miscellaneous or stuff on every box, because you will never open it.

Decluttering before you pack is the single most important cost-saving tip for any move. Moving companies charge by weight or by volume, so the less you have to move, the less you pay. Even if you are doing a DIY move, every box you avoid packing is one less box you have to buy, lift, and unpack. Three months before your move, do a hard edit of every closet, drawer, and shelf. Clothes you have not worn in a year, books you will never read again, kitchen gadgets that take up half a cabinet, and furniture pieces that do not fit your new space should all be sold, donated, or discarded. The earlier you start, the less stressful it is. Selling items on Facebook Marketplace, Poshmark, or local groups can earn you money to offset moving costs. Donating to a local charity or giving away to neighbors is better than hauling unwanted items across town.

Timing your move can cut costs significantly if you have flexibility. Moving companies and truck rental companies charge more on weekends and at the end and beginning of the month, when demand spikes. If you can move on a weekday in the middle of the month, you will save money on both labor and rentals. Weather also affects moving costs — moving on a rainy day is harder, slower, and sometimes more expensive if you need to pay for extra protection for wet furniture. Spring and summer are peak moving seasons, which means everything costs more. If you have the option to move in fall or winter, you will find lower rates and more availability.

Changing your address and transferring services is full of hidden costs if you do not plan ahead. Update your address with the post office as soon as you know you are moving. Most postal services offer mail forwarding for a small monthly fee, which ensures that any mail still addressed to your old address reaches you. Transfer or cancel utilities like electricity, gas, water, internet, and cable a week before you move so you are not paying for services you are not using. Call your current and new internet providers to see if they offer move-in credits or free installation. Many cable and internet companies offer a promotional rate or a waiver of installation fees if you tell them you are considering a competitor because of the move. Always ask for a discount; the worst they can say is no.

On moving day, plan your meals and hydrations carefully. Order pizza for your friends, but also buy water, soda, and snacks in bulk at a grocery store before the move. Buying food at the last minute from gas stations or convenience stores is expensive and unhealthy. If you have kids or pets, arrange for someone to watch them on moving day if possible. Wrangling children or dogs while lifting heavy boxes is dangerous and stressful. A babysitter or a trusted relative for the day costs less than a trip to the emergency room. Pack an essentials box that you carry with you in the car rather than on the truck. This box should contain your charger, medications, toiletries, a change of clothes, your phone charger, important documents, and basic cleaning supplies. You do not want to be digging through labeled boxes at midnight on your first night in the new house trying to find toothpaste.

Deep cleaning your old apartment or house before you leave is the easiest way to get your security deposit back — or at least most of it. Supermarkets and dollar stores sell all-purpose cleaners, sponges, and garbage bags for under fifty reais. Spend a few hours scrubbing the bathrooms, kitchen, and floors before you move out. If you are renting, ask the landlord or property manager for a move-out checklist ahead of time so you know exactly what they expect. If you are selling your house, a deep clean and a few quick repairs like painting walls white, fixing leaky faucets, and replacing broken lightbulbs can increase your sale price by thousands of reais. The cost of supplies is tiny compared to the money you get back.

Setting up your first night in the new place does not have to be overwhelming. Before you move in, order a new mattress protector and bedding online and have it delivered to your new address for the day you arrive. There is nothing worse than sleeping on bare floorboards or a mysterious old mattress your first night. Stock your fridge with easy meals like sandwiches, fruit, pasta, and frozen dinners before you move in, rather than ordering takeout for every meal for the first week. Takeout adds up fast, and having basic groceries makes the first days feel normal rather than chaotic. Hang curtains or temporary blackout shades in your bedroom on day one so you have some privacy. You do not need furniture on day one, but you do need a way to sleep and eat.

Finally, managing the emotional side of moving is as important as saving money. Moving is hard, even when it is a positive change. Give yourself permission to feel sad about leaving your old home, exhausted after moving day, and overwhelmed by the boxes. Budget moves require more of your own labor and time, so you will be more tired than if you paid someone else to do everything. Be kind to yourself. Eat well, sleep when you can, and ask for help when you need it. You do not have to unpack everything in one day. Unpack the essentials first, and then chip away at the rest over the next few weeks. A move that is cheap but destroys your mental health is not worth it. Find the balance between saving money and saving your sanity, and you will have a successful move no matter your budget.
